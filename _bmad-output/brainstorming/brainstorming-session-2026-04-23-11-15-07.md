- **The Problem:** Theoretical Weakness and Uncertainty. The "fog" of vague causal claims that hinders empirical progress.
- **The Goal:** A framework to quantify and systematically reduce theoretical uncertainty.

**III. The Theoretical Multiverse (The "Map of Ignorance")**

- **The "Zones"** as regions of varying theoretical precision.

**IV. Simulation Results (The "Fog-Clearing" Proof)**

**V. Discussion: The Strategic Investment Portfolio**

### Quantification and Investment Logic

**Investment Value Framework:**
- **User-Defined Weights:** Weights for different compatibility criteria are defined by the user based on their specific research goals.
- **Resolution Delta ($\Delta R$):** Value is measured by the number of dyads that move from "Incompatible" $\rightarrow$ "Compatible" when a component's truth is resolved.
- **Investment Value Formula:** $\text{Investment Value}(C) = (\Delta R_{Full} \times W_{Full}) + (\Delta R_{Test} \times W_{Test})$

**Layers of Importance (Hierarchical Investment Map):**
1. **Lynchpin Components (Individual):** High-impact individual components that resolve significant conflicts (low-hanging fruit).
2. **Synergistic Sets (Combinatorial):** Sets of components that, when resolved together, provide a non-linear jump in compatibility (high-leverage breakthroughs).
3. **Architectural Anchors (Structural):** Components/sets that define the boundaries between multiverse zones; resolving these re-organizes the theoretical landscape.

**Narrative Framing:** The results are presented as a "Theoretical Investment Portfolio," distinguishing between high-yield/low-effort (lynchpins) and high-yield/high-effort (synergistic sets) development paths.
